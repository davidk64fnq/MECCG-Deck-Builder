Official Card Attributes Reference

Every card in the catalog carries official rules attributes synchronized directly from the Cardnum open database. Depending on the attribute, values can be filtered in the lower panel (Slots 1 & 2), queried in procedural deck rules, or inspected in the right-click Card Attributes monospace view.

Illustrative Values Notice: The values listed throughout this reference are representative examples drawn from the seven official Iron Crown Enterprises (I.C.E.) expansion sets (The Wizards, The Dragons, Dark Minions, The Lidless Eye, Against the Shadow, The White Hand, and The Balrog). They are not intended to be exhaustive closed lists. Because Cardnum syncs open community data, you will also encounter specialized traits, set-specific havens (e.g. Wizardhaven), and composite shorthand codes (e.g. B, B F) in the dropdowns.

2. Official Gameplay Attributes (Dropdown Slots 1 & 2)

The following 29 attributes represent official rules criteria. All 29 appear in alphabetical order in the Attribute dropdowns of Filter Slots 1 & 2. In the right-click Card Attributes menu, any of these attributes that carry a non-empty value on the selected card are displayed in the monospace readout.

Stat Modifiers, Relational Operators & [Blank] Filtering:
Plain values represent direct, unconditional modifiers to the attribute itself. In procedural deck generation rules, numeric attributes (Prowess, Body, Mind, Direct, Corruption, MPs, Strikes) support relational operators (>, >=, <, <=, !=), range bounding (e.g. "Mind": ">= 3; <= 5"), and sentinel blank checks ("[Blank]").
Filtering for Unset Fields: Whenever matching cards in scope lack an attribute value (such as characters without a Home site or items without Corruption), a dedicated [Blank] sentinel appears at the top of the Value dropdown.
Attribute Polarity: Modifiers reflect net impact on the stat total—most notably in Corruption, where character resistance bonuses (e.g. +2 printed on Hobbits) are indexed as negative numbers (-2) because they reduce the character's net corruption burden.

3. Data Provenance & Database Discrepancies

Understanding how card data is modeled and synchronized helps explain unexpected query results, avoid lost edits, and ensure your procedural deck rules remain robust:

A. Cardnum Open Database Synchronization & Modeling Quirks

The catalog synchronizes on application startup from the community-maintained Cardnum repository (cards-dc.json). Because Cardnum was compiled from disparate community databases over many years, several attributes reflect historical database modeling decisions rather than literal transcriptions of printed card text:

  • The Corruption Polarity Inversion (Hobbit Resistance): On character cards, the oval in the lower-right corner displays a bonus to corruption check rolls (for example, Folco Boffin, Merry, Pippin, and Robin Smallburrow all literally display +2, while Frodo displays +4). However, Cardnum unifies the Corruption field across all card types to represent net corruption burden:
    • Items and hazards that inflict corruption points (CP) are positive (e.g. 1, 2, 3 CP).
    • Character resistance bonuses that reduce the risk of corruption are indexed as negative values (e.g. -2 for Hobbits, -4 for Frodo) because mathematically, +2 to the check roll equals -2 to the net corruption burden.
    • To find corruption-resistant characters, filter for negative values like -2 or query "Corruption": "< 0" in procedural generation rules.
  • Parenthesized Values: Values in parentheses (such as (1) or (-1)) indicate conditional modifiers (e.g., Warrior only on Valiant Sword, or Éowyn only on Helm of Her Secrecy) or direct dice roll check adjustments. The procedural engine's numeric parser automatically strips parentheses to evaluate the underlying stat cleanly without error.
  • Creature Race Normalization & Species Exceptions: Cardnum originally split creature species between singular unique individuals (Troll for Bert) and plural packs (Trolls for Olog-hai), along with composite strings (Wolf Wolves, Man Men, Drake Sand-drake). MECCG Deck Builder normalizes all creature races on startup to singular nouns (Troll, Orc, Spider, Wolf, Animal, Dragon, Drake):
    • Cave-drake as a Dragon: Although titled with "-drake", Cave-drake (METW/MELE) prints the literal rules keyword Dragon. (published before the Drake keyword was introduced in METD) and is classified under Race: Dragon per official ICE and Council of Elrond tournament rulings.
    • Awakened Plant vs. Ent: Hazard creatures (*Old Man Willow*, *Huorn*, *Ent in Search of the Entwives*) literally print the keyword Awakened Plant. and are classified as Race: Awakened Plant to align with Wellinghall's automatic attack, while Race: Ent is reserved for Hero characters, allies, and factions.
    • Multi-Species Creatures: Creatures with multiple printed keywords (such as Beorning Skin-changers with Animals. Men. Bears.) are indexed as composite multi-values (Animal; Man; Bear), allowing them to match under any of their individual component species.
  • Creature Traits in Skills: Cardnum stores hazard tactical traits (such as Flying, Ringwraith, Undead, or Drake) in the Skill field alongside character classes (Warrior, Scout, etc.).
  • Shorthand Codes: Site cards occasionally feature legacy shorthand classifications, such as B (Border-hold), B F (Border-hold/Free-hold), or Wizardhaven (Fallen-wizard havens from The White Hand).
  • Composite Playable Strings vs. Discrete Item Flags: On physical site cards, playable entries are comma-separated (e.g., "Playable: Information, Items (minor)"). In Cardnum's raw database (cards-dc.json), category commas were omitted, resulting in run-on strings like "Information Items (minor)" or "Scroll of Isildur Palantiri Items (minor, major)".
    • Because the application indexes official attributes directly, each unique permutation appears as a single dropdown item in Filter Slots 1 & 2.
    • Cardnum already decomposed all playability criteria into individual boolean flag attributes: MinorItem, MajorItem, GreaterItem, GoldRing, Information, Palantiri, Scroll, and Hoard.
    • For procedural deck rules and filter searches, query the specific attribute (e.g., "MinorItem": "yes" or "Information": "yes") rather than matching against the composite Playable string.

B. Curated Local Master Dataset & Data Persistence

MECCG Deck Builder ships with a curated, validated master dataset (cards-dc.json) loaded directly from local storage on launch. The application does not contact remote servers on startup, so it will never overwrite your local file.

Any manual corrections or customizations made directly to cards-dc.json persist permanently across sessions and restarts.

C. Recommended Workaround: Custom Categories & Tags

The cleanest, permanent way to handle missing or unintuitive official attributes is to create local taxonomy tags:

  1. Right-click the card and select Assign Tag > [+ Author New Tag...] (or open Taxonomy > Manage Custom Tags...).
  2. Enter your preferred category and tag (for example, Corruption Resistance: +2, Combat Role: Scout Support, or Skill Fix: Sage).
  3. Filter by these terms using Custom Tags (Slots 3 & 4) in the filter bar.
  4. Back up your assignments at any time via Taxonomy > Save Tag Set... so they can be preserved or shared across machines.

D. Authoring Robust Procedural Deck Rules

Because upstream open databases are maintained by community volunteers and could experience structural updates (or transition to alternative mirrors such as Play MECCG), author your procedural generation JSON templates defensively:

  • Use Numeric Operators: Query ranges and stat thresholds directly (e.g. "Mind": ">= 3; <= 5" or "Prowess": ">= 6") rather than enumerating every possible integer.
  • Use Semicolon OR Logic: Match multiple related variations where appropriate (e.g., "Skill": "Diplomat; Sage" or "Corruption": "-2; -4").
  • Query Custom Tags When Appropriate: If a strategy relies on roles or classifications that Cardnum represents inconsistently, tag the cards yourself and query your user taxonomy using "Tags": { "Archetype": "Ring Destruction" } instead of relying solely on "Attributes".
  • Always Provide Fallbacks: Attach a nested Fallback rule to any query that depends on specific database strings. If an upstream attribute value shifts, the engine will detect the shortfall and execute the fallback automatically, ensuring your deck remains complete and legal.
