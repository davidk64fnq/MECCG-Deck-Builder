Companion Rules Tag Set Reference

MECCG Deck Builder utilizes a two-tier database architecture on startup, loading two distinct local files with zero network latency:

  • cards-dc.json (Core Catalog): The official card database containing printed rules statistics (Primary, Alignment, Race, Skills, Mind, Prowess, etc.), with normalized creature species and structured, machine-actionable Home site predicates.
  • Rules_Support_Tags.json (Rules Intelligence Layer): A companion taxonomy dataset authored to bridge unstructured card text into queryable taxonomy tags—mapping site automatic attacks, character recruiting bonuses, and event requirements so that generic procedural deck rules can generate legal, themed decks automatically.
Jump to Category: Adjacent Regions • Adjacent Sites • Automatic Attack • Combat Bonus • Environment Stance • Faction Bonus • Home Region • Prerequisite • Prerequisite For • Region Requirement • Region Type • Role • Site Requirement • Target Item • Target Species
Pre-Packaged Developer Baseline: All categories and tags listed in this reference are already completed and pre-packaged. You do not need to run any extractions or tag any cards—the procedural deck generator and filter dropdowns can use these categories immediately upon opening the app.
Taxonomy Design Philosophy — Structural Hooks vs. In-Game Modifiers:
The companion taxonomy dataset does not attempt to transcribe every situational penalty, dice modifier, or flavor handicap printed on card faces. (Cards already display those rules when in hand or on the table).

Instead, tags serve strictly as structural deck-building bridges between disparate card types:
  • Positive Strategic Enablers: Tags capture abilities that procedural rules actively search for—such as recruiting a faction (Faction Bonus), fighting a specific monster (Combat Bonus), or pairing a quest with its required enabler card (Prerequisite).
  • Why Negative Drawbacks are Omitted (e.g., Barliman Butterbur): Characters with situational handicaps (such as Barliman's "-1 to influence checks against factions") are never drafted for their penalties. They are drafted for their baseline stats (e.g. Mind: 1, Skill: Warrior, Home: Bree). Tagging negative modifiers would clutter the taxonomy with dead values that no procedural deck rule would ever query.
  • Avoiding Stat Duplication: Ratings already tracked in the official database (such as base Direct, Prowess, Mind, or official Corruption burden) are not duplicated as custom tags. Deck rules query those printed properties directly.
Card Examples & Set Conventions: Wherever possible, card examples throughout this guide are drawn from the base premiere set, The Wizards (METW). For gameplay mechanics introduced in subsequent expansions (such as Under-deeps sites introduced in The Dragons or minion/fallen mechanics from The Lidless Eye and The Balrog), the expansion set code is explicitly noted in parentheses (e.g. MEBA).

Where to Store Your Own Custom Tags

If you create your own taxonomy categories or tags using the Tag Manager or Extraction Tools, you have two choices for where to store them:

  • Option 1: Merge into the Auto-Loaded Baseline (Rules_Support_Tags.json): If you want your custom categories to load automatically every time you launch the application, choose Taxonomy > Save Tag Set..., select Rules_Support_Tags.json, and confirm overwrite.
  • Option 2: Save to a Modular Tag File (e.g. Tournament_Hazards.json): If you are creating specialized strategies or sharing tag sets with other players, save them to a standalone .json file. These files will not load automatically on launch; you pull them into your active session on demand via Taxonomy > Load Tag Set... or Taxonomy > Merge Tag Set....

2. Detailed Category Reference

Adjacent Regions

Card Scope: Region (Region cards only; sites strictly remain [Blank])

Records all physical territories sharing a contiguous border with this region. In the application's engine, this category forms the in-memory adjacency graph of Middle-earth:

  • Bidirectional Integrity: Border connections are bidirectional (if Region A borders Region B, Region B borders Region A). The BFS pathfinding graph builder validates and enforces this symmetry automatically.
  • Overland Hiking Routes: When authoring Geographic Theater rules with "IncludeOverlandPath": true, the engine uses this graph layer to compute the shortest contiguous highway from Rhudaur (Rivendell) to any operational theater across the map.
  • Contiguous Cluster Expansion: Allows roaming theater rules to expand an adjacent territorial cluster of 3 to 6 regions seeded directly from a haven's home territory.
Representative Examples:
  • Angmar; Cardolan; Forochel; Lindon; Númeriador; Rhudaur; The Shire → Borders for Arthedain
  • Anduin Vales; Dagorlad; Southern Mirkwood; Wold & Foothills → Borders for Brown Lands
  • Anfalas; Bay of Belfalas; Lamedon; Lebennin; Mouths of the Anduin → Borders for Belfalas
  • Gorgoroth → Single border connection for Udûn

Adjacent Sites

Card Scope: Site (Under-deeps sites only; surface sites strictly remain [Blank])

Records all physical locations directly accessible from an Under-deeps site (introduced in The Dragons, The Lidless Eye, and The Balrog)—including both surface site entrances and contiguous Under-deeps sites—with passage movement difficulty ratings stripped so they can be matched cleanly as card titles.

The "No Orphans" Rule: An Under-deeps site drafted without its corresponding surface entrance is completely unplayable in game—your company can never travel there.

Because all 69 sites in The Wizards (METW) are surface sites or Havens, they strictly leave Adjacent Sites [Blank]. This allows procedural rules to draft any deep lair and then query "Adjacent Sites": "[Blank]" to auto-draft its surface entrance into the site deck with zero risk of broken travel paths.
Representative Examples:
  • Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold; Remains of Thangorodrim; The Under-vaults → Connected surface and Under-deeps sites for The Drowning-deeps (MEBA)
  • Barad-dûr; The Under-galleries; The Under-leas → Connected surface and Under-deeps sites for The Under-gates (MELE)
  • [Blank] → All 69 surface sites and Havens in The Wizards (METW)

Automatic Attack

Card Scope: Site

Indexes guardian creature species printed on site cards using normalized singular terms (e.g. Orc, not Orcs) to align directly with hazard creature race attributes. Peaceful holds and Havens leave this field [Blank]. For sites that allow open attacks (e.g. "opponent may play any creature keyed to Ruins & Lairs"), dynamic site predicates (e.g. Site=Ruins & Lairs) define which hazard creatures can attack there.

Sites vs. Events/Hazards: Automatic Attack is reserved strictly for site cards to record their native guardians. For resource or hazard events that create, modify, or cancel automatic attacks (such as Siege, Quiet Lands, or Sun), use Role: Automatic Attack.
Representative Examples:
  • Dragon → Dancing Spire, The Lonely Mountain, Caves of Ûlund, Irerock
  • Orc; Troll → Dual-attack fortresses Barad-dûr and Dol Guldur
  • Orc → Carn Dûm, Cirith Ungol, Goblin-gate, Moria, Mount Gram, Shelob's Lair
  • Pûkel-creature → Glittering Caves, Stone-circle, The Stones
  • Spider → Ruined Signal Tower
  • Troll → Ettenmoors
  • Undead → Amon Hen, Barrow-downs, Dead Marshes, Gladden Fields, Minas Morgul, Tolfalas
  • Wolf → Isengard, Ost-in-Edhil, The White Towers, Weathertop
  • Site=Ruins & Lairs; Site=Shadow-hold → Framsburg (METD — dynamic hold predicate)
  • [Blank] → All 4 Havens and 30 peaceful Free-holds and Border-holds (Bag End, Bree, Edoras, Minas Tirith, Rivendell, Wellinghall)

Combat Bonus

Card Scope: Character

Flags characters providing martial advantages—such as direct prowess bonuses, body/survival bonuses, opponent body reduction, or strike roll modifiers—specifically against targeted creature species. Left blank for general combatants without species-specific rules text.

Characters vs. Non-Characters: Combat Bonus is reserved strictly for characters. For items, weapons, events, or hazards that modify combat or target specific creature types, use Role: Combat (the mechanism) and Target Species (the targeted species).
Representative Examples:
  • Orc → Character prowess and strike check bonuses on Dáin II and Gimli
  • Nazgûl → Character combat prowess superiority on Éowyn

Environment Stance

Card Scope: Resource • Hazard (Events & Creatures)

Tracks whether a card requires or benefits from Gates of Morning (Light) or Doors of Night (Shadow) being in play. (Items and allies do not use this tag, as environmental conditions only affect events and nocturnal creatures.) This supports two primary workflows:

  • Dedicated Strategy (Top-Down): When building a dedicated Gates of Morning daylight deck or Doors of Night dark weather hazard suite, filtering by this category quickly gathers all matching cards.
  • Automated Prerequisite Support (Bottom-Up): When procedural deck rules draft cards that depend on day or night, the generator can automatically pull Gates of Morning or Doors of Night into the deck so those cards can legally enter play.
Stance Values:
  • Gates of Morning → Light-dependent cards (Gates of Morning, Clear Skies, Fog, Dark Quarrels, Fair Travels in Dark-domains, Fair Travels in Shadow-lands, Quiet Lands, Stars, Sun, The Cock Crows, The Evenstar)
  • Doors of Night → Shadow-dependent cards (Doors of Night, Foul Fumes, Long Winter, Morgul Night, Snowstorm, Gloom, Choking Shadows, Shelob, The Will of Sauron, and all four Unique Dragons)
  • Gates of Morning; Doors of Night → Dual-environment lunar cards (Moon)
  • [Blank] → Standard open events and creatures playable in clear weather

Faction Bonus

Card Scope: Character

Tracks character Direct Influence (DI) bonuses for recruiting specific named factions or entire species of factions. In procedural deck generation, querying "Name": "{Leader.Category:Faction Bonus}" automatically drafts the exact faction your company leader specializes in recruiting.

Faction Recruitment vs. Follower Influence (e.g., Balin, Dáin II):
Several diplomats feature printed text granting direct influence bonuses against both a faction and individual characters of that species (for example, Balin reads "+1 direct influence against Dwarves and Dwarf factions").
  • Why Factions are Tagged: Factions are external resource cards that must be deliberately included in your play deck. Faction Bonus provides the machine-actionable hook needed by procedural rules to pull that faction into the deck.
  • Why Follower Influence is Omitted: Controlling followers in a company is governed by a character's printed Direct attribute and biological Race. Procedural company recruitment rules already draft companions by race (e.g. "Race": "{Leader.Race}") or DI capacity (e.g. "Direct": ">= 2"). Tagging Balin with follower influence would duplicate existing official attributes without adding functional deck-construction value.
Representative Examples:
  • Wood-elves → Specific named faction recruited with bonus by Thranduil
  • Iron Hill Dwarves; Race=Man → Named faction and species recruitment bonuses on Dáin II
  • Riders of Rohan → Specific named faction recruited with bonus by Théoden
  • Dunlendings → Specific named faction recruited with bonus by Peath
  • All → Universal diplomat wildcard matching any faction in active expansions (Adrazar)

Card Scope: Resource (Quests & Multi-Card Storylines)

Prerequisite For

Card Scope: Resource (Enabler Events & Quest Relics)

The inverse companion to Prerequisite. Identifies which quest or storyline card this card enables.

The Enabler Shield Pattern: When drafting generic utility cards, querying "Prerequisite For": "[Blank]" guarantees that niche enablers (like Paths of the Dead or Reforging) are never drafted as dead cards into decks that lack their parent quest.

Region Requirement

Card Scope: Hazard • Resource

Consolidates all card-to-region playability constraints and modifications into a single queryable category. Identifies:

  1. Geographic territories required to bring a card into play (e.g. hazard creatures keyed to specific regions, or coastal mounts), and
  2. Territories an ongoing event explicitly buffs, alters, or affects (e.g. regional combat modifiers or Ranger movement shortcuts).
Dual-Mode Cards (Creature / Event): For cards with dual card types (such as the Nazgûl), only tag the specific regions required to play them as a creature. Do not tag All to represent their event mode being globally playable—doing so would allow the deck generator to falsely draft the card as a creature in illegal regions (such as the Shire).
Representative Examples:
  • Harondor; Horse Plains; Gorgoroth; Khand → Playable creature territories for Akhôrahil
  • Imlad Morgul; Gorgoroth → Playable creature territories for Shelob
  • Anórien; Dagorlad; Harondor; Ithilien; Lebennin; Rohan → Transit regions for Anduin River
  • Dagorlad; Gorgoroth; Horse Plains → Transit regions for Ash Mountains
  • Anduin Vales; Dunland; Fangorn; Hollin; Rhudaur; Wold & Foothills → Transit regions for Misty Mountains
  • Gorgoroth; Harondor; Ithilien; Nurn → Transit regions for Mountains of Shadow
  • Anfalas; Anórien; Lamedon; Old Pûkel-land; Rohan → Transit regions for White Mountains
  • Dagorlad; Udûn → Infiltration path modified by Morannon (Permanent-event)
  • Hollin; Redhorn Gate; Wold & Foothills → Regional attacks buffed by Balrog of Moria (Permanent-event)
  • Wilderness; Border-land → Terrain required for travel hazards (Snowstorm, Long Winter)
  • Coastal Sea → Maritime travel boundaries for Fair Sailing and Great Ship

Role

Card Scope: Resource • Hazard

Classifies the primary tactical game mechanism executed by non-character cards (events, items, hazards, allies). Symmetrical across alignments—the card's Primary type dictates whether the effect aids your company or disrupts the opponent.

Custom Roles: The 14 roles listed below represent the pre-packaged baseline taxonomy shipped in Rules_Support_Tags.json. You can define additional custom roles (such as Scout Support or Nazgûl Hunter) via Taxonomy > Manage Custom Tags... at any time.
Baseline Roles (Pre-packaged Dataset):
  • Automatic Attack — Modifies, buffs, cancels, or creates site guardian attacks faced by the whole company (Balrog of Moria, Siege, Quiet Lands, Sun). Note: Cards or allies with personal immunity to automatic-attack strikes (such as Treebeard or Goldberry) are classified under Role: Protection.
  • Card Flow — Managing cards and intelligence across the hand, deck, and discard pipeline—drawing extra cards (Favor of the Valar), scouting or inspecting hands and draw decks (Far-sight, Mirror of Galadriel, Palantír of Amon Sûl), filtering and reordering play decks (Palantír of Annúminas), retrieving cards from the discard pile (Mouth of Sauron, Reforging, Vilya), mass hazard permanent-event discard (Gates of Morning, The Cock Crows, Wizard's River-horses), expanding maximum hand size (Smoke Rings), or attacking and forcing opponent discards (Khamûl the Easterling, Drowning Seas).
  • Combat — Engaging in physical combat—boosting prowess or body during an attack, adjusting combat dice rolls, adding strikes, or triggering bonus attacks:
    • Company Martial Strength (Resources): Prowess buffs and combat tricks used by characters when fighting strikes (Block, Dodge, Risky Blow, Lucky Strike, Glamdring, Clear Skies, Fellowship, Kindling of the Spirit, Sacrifice of Form, Stars, Sun, The Evenstar, The Old Thrush, True Fána, Wizard's Fire, Wizard's Flame).
    • Attack Escalation & Lethality (Hazards): Buffing creature prowess, increasing strike counts, or triggering extra attacks against companies (Wake of War, Minions Stir, Orc-guard, Traitor, The Pale Sword).
  • Corruption — Strategic corruption offense or defense—forces corruption checks or burdens (Lure of Power, Despair of the Heart), mitigates and heals corruption (Praise to Elbereth, Elf-song, Palantír of Elostirion), or grants roll bonuses and auto-passes (A Friend or Three, Fellowship, Halfling Strength, Nenya, New Friendship, Old Friendship).
  • Duration — Extends the life or duration of active events in play (The Will of Sauron).
  • Influence — Modifies mathematical influence totals, control ratings, or influence check rolls:
    • Stat & Capacity Adjustments: Direct Influence (DI) ratings, general influence pools, or character control limits (Return of the King, Nenya, Wizard's Voice).
    • Influence Check Modifiers: Numerical bonuses, roll rerolls, or penalties to influence dice rolls made against characters, followers, or factions (Lordly Presence, Old Friendship, New Friendship, A Friend or Three, Persuasive Words, Tempering Friendship, Muster).
    • Influence Defense: Cancelling influence attempts made by opponent companies or agents (Wizard's Laughter).
  • Item — Interacts with physical gear—retrieving items from discard (Reforging), searching play decks (Lucky Search), playing items at tapped sites (Thorough Search), activating seeing stones (Align Palantír, Use Palantír), testing Gold Rings (Ringlore, Test of Form, Test of Lore, Wizard's Test, Scroll of Isildur, Dwarven Rings), or forcing the theft/discard of items (Indûr Dawndeath, Pick-pocket, Thief).
  • Movement — Modifies travel, region pathing, multi-site journeys, haven return, or forces company retreat (Anduin River, Ash Mountains, Bridge, Great-road, Misty Mountains, Morannon, Mountains of Shadow, White Mountains, Foul Fumes, Long Winter).
  • Protection — Mitigating or avoiding danger during combat—cancelling attacks before strikes are rolled, dodging strikes, granting strike immunity, or preventing wounds:
    • Attack & Strike Evasion: Cancelling attacks before strikes are rolled, slipping away unseen, or dodging specific terrain strikes (Concealment, Dark Quarrels, Escape, Ford, Halfling Stealth, Hiding, Praise to Elbereth, Sacrifice of Form, Secret Entrance, Secret Passage, Stealth, The Cock Crows, Vanishment, Wizard's River-horses, Elven Cloak, Torque of Hues, Magic Ring of Nature, Magic Ring of Stealth).
    • Immunity & Target Denial: Allies or characters that cannot be attacked or chosen as strike targets (Treebeard, Goldberry, Leaflock, Tom Bombadil).
    • Combat Survival: Shielding characters from being wounded or preventing discard when defeated (Halfling Strength, Sacrifice of Form).
    • Hazard Limit Reduction: Decreasing hazard limit draws on company movement (Eagle-mounts, Fair Sailing, Fair Travels..., Great Ship, Horses).
  • Quest — Directly advances victory conditions or scenario goals by awarding Marshalling Points (MPs) for completing specific site journeys, storing relics, or destroying The One Ring (Andúril, Cracks of Doom, Dreams of Lore, Gollum's Fate, Rescue Prisoners, Stone of Erech, Earth of Galadriel's Orchard). Non-scoring utility cards that transform the board (such as The White Tree) are classified under their functional mechanism (e.g. Role: Site).
  • Readiness — Character and ally stamina, action capability, and physical condition—tapping, untapping, recovering from wounds, or returning characters to hand:
    • Wound Recovery & Healing: Transitioning characters from wounded to well (Athelas, Healing Herbs, Halfling Strength).
    • Stamina & Untapping: Untapping characters outside the untap phase or refreshing exhausted companions (Andúril, Halfling Strength, Narya, The Arkenstone).
    • Adversary Disruption: Forcing characters to tap or bouncing them back to hand (Adûnaphel, Call of Home).
  • Recruitment — Enables, accelerates, or alters the physical entry of characters and factions into play (beyond numerical check modifiers):
    • Location & Site Bypasses: Playing characters or factions at non-standard sites or alternative havens (A Chance Meeting, Old Road).
    • Alternative Recruitment Engines: Enlisting factions through special substitute rules rather than standard diplomat influence checks (Muster, Roäc the Raven, The Great Eagles).
    • Recruitment Pacing: Playing extra characters in a turn or tutoring characters/factions directly from deck or discard.
  • Site — Alters site status, hold types, or site playability (Morannon, Quiet Lands, The White Tree, Witch-king of Angmar).
  • Terrain — Converts region path symbols and territory terrain classifications between hostile and safe (Fog, Moon, Quiet Lands, Stars, The Evenstar, Morgul Night).

Site Requirement

Card Scope: Resource • Hazard

Consolidates all card-to-site relationships into a single queryable category. Identifies:

  1. The specific site(s) or hold type(s) where a company must be located to play the card (items, allies, factions, and site-phase events), and
  2. The specific site(s) or hold type(s) an event attaches to, targets, or modifies.

Supports specific named sites (e.g. Minas Tirith, Moria), structural site hold classifications (e.g. Dark-hold, Shadow-hold, Ruins & Lairs, Free-hold, Border-hold, Haven), and dynamic site predicates (e.g. Information!=[Blank]).

Modal "OR" Events vs. Hard Prerequisites:
Only tag Site Requirement if the site or hold type is a mandatory prerequisite required to play the card (e.g. Rescue Prisoners requiring a Dark-hold or Shadow-hold, or Bridge requiring a Haven).

If an event features an unconstrained primary mode alongside an optional keying branch (such as Dragon's Desolation, which offers "+2 prowess to any Dragon attack OR play a Dragon at Ruins & Lairs"), leave Site Requirement [Blank]. Tagging the conditional enabler would falsely brand the card as a site-locked hazard in procedural rules, preventing general decks from drafting it as open combat support.
Representative Examples:
  • Haven → Mandatory entry origin or arrival haven (Bridge, Fellowship, Great-road, Old Road)
  • Minas Tirith → Target attachment and scoring stronghold (Return of the King, The White Tree)
  • Mount Doom → Climax site for Ring destruction quests (Cracks of Doom, Gollum's Fate)
  • Vale of Erech → Staging site for Stone of Erech and Army of the Dead
  • Dunharrow → Expedition starting site for Paths of the Dead
  • Lórien → Mandatory scrying site for Mirror of Galadriel
  • Eagles' Eyrie → Mandatory flight origin for Eagle-mounts
  • Border-hold; Free-hold; Ruins & Lairs → Permitted recruitment holds for A Chance Meeting
  • Border-hold; Dark-hold; Free-hold; Shadow-hold → Holding holds for Rescue Prisoners
  • Information!=[Blank] → Dynamic predicate matching any site where Information is playable for Sage lore rituals (Andúril, Dreams of Lore, Far-sight, Reforging, Ringlore)
  • Border-hold; Free-hold → Target holds for Siege
  • Ruins & Lairs → Target holds for Awaken Denizens
  • Moria; Lórien → Target sites for Balrog of Moria
  • [Blank] → Standard open combat, travel, and protection events playable anywhere

Target Item

Card Scope: Resource • Hazard

Identifies general item classifications, gear types, or rules keywords (e.g. Weapon, Armor, Gold Ring, Palantír, Lost Knowledge) that an event or hazard requires, enhances, transfers, tests, or targets for theft/corruption.

The Ring Hierarchy — Gold Ring vs. Ring:
Taxonomy tags maintain a strict functional boundary across ring mechanics:
  • Target Item: Gold Ring (The Testing Suite): Reserved exclusively for cards that interact with or test untested Gold Rings (Ringlore, Scroll of Isildur, Test of Form, Test of Lore, Wizard's Test). This aligns directly with the official site attribute GoldRing: yes.
  • Target Item: Ring (Universal Ring Hazards): Assigned to hazards that target, punish, or corrupt a character bearing any ring (Gold Ring, Magic Ring, Lesser Ring, Dwarven Ring, or The One Ring), such as The Precious, The Ring's Betrayal, and The Will of the Ring.
  • Specific One Ring Quests: Quests specifically mandating the destruction of The One Ring (such as Cracks of Doom or Gollum's Fate) declare Prerequisite: The One Ring rather than a broad category tag.
Representative Examples:
  • Gold Ring → Untested rings and testing rituals (Ringlore, Scroll of Isildur, Test of Form, Test of Lore, Wizard's Test)
  • Ring → Universal ring corruption hazards (The Precious, The Ring's Betrayal, The Will of the Ring, The Nazgûl are Abroad)
  • Palantír → Seeing stone activation and corruption (Align Palantír, Use Palantír, Bane of the Ithil-stone)
  • Armor; Weapon → Martial gear retrieved by Reforging

Target Species

Card Scope: Resource (Weapons, Items, Allies, Events) • Hazard

Identifies specific biological species or creature races (e.g. Elf, Dúnadan, Wizard, Nazgûl, Orc) that an event, weapon, item, ally, or hazard explicitly targets, modifies, or aids. Supports exact species names as well as dynamic exclusion predicates.

Named Characters vs. Species: Do not use this category for specific named individuals (such as Aragorn II or Gandalf). Named character requirements are official database properties tracked directly under the Specific attribute (e.g. Specific: Aragorn II on Return of the King, or Specific: Gandalf on Narya).
Representative Examples:
  • Wizard → Mandatory caster race for Istari spells (Kindling of the Spirit, Sacrifice of Form, True Fána, Vanishment, Wizard's Fire, Wizard's Flame, Wizard's Laughter, Wizard's River-horses, Wizard's Voice, Wizard's Test)
  • Hobbit → Exclusive evasion and endurance perks (Halfling Stealth, Halfling Strength)
  • Elf → Species prowess buff on The Evenstar
  • Dúnadan; Man → Daytime combat prowess buff on Sun
  • Dúnadan → Bearer untapping stamina on Andúril, the Flame of the West
  • Man; Orc; Troll → Attack cancellation on Dark Quarrels
  • Troll → Attack cancellation on The Cock Crows
  • Nazgûl → Event/attack cancellation on Praise to Elbereth and Wizard's River-horses
  • Race!=Nazgûl → Printed exclusion on The Old Thrush (cannot target Nazgûl attacks)
  • Orc; Troll → Minion swarm attack buffs (Minions Stir, Wake of War, Fury of the Iron Crown)
  • Undead → Attack escalation for spirits and wights (Plague of Wights)
  • Race!=Hobbit && Race!=Wizard → Printed character exclusions on corruption hazards (Despair of the Heart, Lure of Nature, Lure of Power, Greed)
